For EHDI program coordinators and data staff

A free, standard infant hearing report for any state

Audiologists could use this form to send infant diagnostic results to your Early Hearing Detection and Intervention (EHDI) program. We're asking programs to review it.

Review draft. No state EHDI program has accepted this report yet, so we're asking programs to look at it before any audiologist uses it. Every printed page of the report says it's a draft.

Why

Each state has its own reporting form, with its own labels and answer choices. An audiologist who sees children from more than one state has to keep track of several forms. Some results never get reported at all. In a national survey of 1,024 audiology facilities, the most common reason for not reporting was not knowing how (60%).

Chung W, et al. Reporting newborn audiologic results to state EHDI programs. Ear & Hearing 2017;38(5):638–642.

How we built it

  • We read the diagnostic reporting forms and rules for all 50 states and DC.
  • We brought the fields from those forms into one standard set, with the same names, order, and answer choices for all 50 states and DC.
  • The terms follow JCIH 2019 and ASHA. A pediatric audiologist reviewed every field.

What's on the report

  • Everything used to match records sits together: other names the child may be filed under, the birth mother's name, and record numbers.
  • Each ear gets hearing status, type, permanence, and degree. Degree is printed with its dB range, and it's based on the worst threshold from 500 to 4000 Hz.
  • Every question has a "not yet determined" choice. Congenital CMV has its own line.
  • Page 2 starts with the follow-up question: continue monitoring, or no further follow-up needed.
  • The report prints your program's fax, email, or portal and the deadline, along with where that came from and when it was last checked.
A sample report. It's two US Letter pages. An optional third page adds risk indicators (JCIH 2019), test results by ear, and parent permission to share results.

How it works

  • It's one file. It opens in any web browser and prints to PDF. There's nothing to install and no account to make.
  • What the audiologist types stays on their computer. There's no server.
  • It's meant to be used along with your own form or online system. If your state uses a portal, staff can type from the printout.
  • It's free and open source (MIT license).

What we're asking your program

  1. Would you accept this report by fax or email, or as a printout your staff can type from?
  2. If not, what would need to change?
  3. Is your state's entry correct: how to send it, where, and the deadline?
